title: Bandwidth
description: The download and upload capacity of your line, the speed limits, the units njMule uses and how the limits work with the speed sliders and the scheduler.

The **Bandwidth** page says what your line can carry and how much of it njMule may use. The
**capacities** describe your line: they limit nothing, but set the top of the speed sliders. The
**limits** are what njMule really keeps to. The web interface has the same page.

Every option of this page applies at once and is kept in `njmule.properties`, in whole KiB/s.

## Capacities

| Option | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Download capacity of the line** | 83.9 Mbit/s (10,240 KB/s) | What your line can download, typed in Mbit/s as providers sell it; beside it njMule shows the same speed in bytes (≈ 10.00 MB/s). The top of the download slider, and what **Full speed** sets the download limit to. From 1 KB/s to 10,000,000 KB/s. Key `limits.downloadCapacityKiBps`. Also in the web interface. |
| **Upload capacity of the line** | 8.4 Mbit/s (1,024 KB/s) | What your line can upload, in Mbit/s; the top of the upload slider, and what **Full speed** sets the upload limit to. Same range. Key `limits.uploadCapacityKiBps`. Also in the web interface. |

The defaults stand for a common home line, about 80 Mbit/s down and 8 up. The first-start wizard
offers presets for them, and the [import from eMule](../getting-started/import-emule.mdx) takes
eMule's capacities.

## Limits

| Option | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Download limit** | **No limit** | The most njMule downloads, all downloads together: a number in **KB/s** or **MB/s**, decimals allowed, or **No limit**. From 1 KB/s to 10,000,000 KB/s. Key `limits.downloadKiBps`, `0` for no limit. Also in the web interface. |
| **Upload limit** | **No limit** | The most njMule uploads, all uploads together, in the same way. The number of upload slots follows it. Key `limits.uploadKiBps`, `0` for no limit. Also in the web interface. |

Leave some of your upload free: a line whose upload is full slows its downloads down too, because
the acknowledgements of the data you receive have to go up the same line.

## Units

| Unit | Means |
| --- | --- |
| **KB/s** | 1,024 bytes a second (a KiB/s) |
| **MB/s** | 1,024 KB/s |
| **Mbit/s** | 1,000,000 bits a second, that is 125,000 bytes a second |

A speed typed in another unit is kept in whole KiB/s, rounded. Choosing another unit in the list
writes the same speed again in it, so `1` MB/s becomes `1024` KB/s.

## Limits elsewhere

The same limits are set from other places, and every change shows on this page at once:

- the **Speed limits** under the rates of the top bar, and over the limits of the status strip;
- the menu of the icon in the Windows notification area;
- the remote control: `njmule --remote limits 50 1M` sets the upload and download limits (see
  [Remote control](../server/remote-control.mdx)).

<Screenshot
  id="topbar-limits"
  alt="The Speed limits pop-up under the rates of the top bar: a Download slider at No limit, an Upload slider at 250.0 KB/s, and the buttons Full speed and Slow down."
  caption="The speed sliders: their top is the capacity of the line."
/>

Each slider runs from no limit up to the capacity of the line, or up to the limit when you set one
higher than the capacity. **Full speed** sets both limits to the capacities, **Slow down** to 1 KB/s
each.

While a [scheduler](scheduler.mdx) rule holds, the limits it sets replace these; the values of this
page stay as they are and apply again when no rule holds.
