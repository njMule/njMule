The **Messages** page says who may send you chat messages and what is filtered out: messages holding
words you list, messages from anyone but your friends, and the usual chat spam. A message left out
never shows, and runs no [program](events.mdx) and sends no [notification](notifications.mdx). The
web interface has the same page.

A client counts as your friend only when it is in your friends list and its identity is proven by
secure identification: anyone could send a friend's user hash.

Every option of this page is kept in `njmule.properties` and applies at once. Chatting is described
on [Messages](../desktop/messages.mdx).

## Options

| Option | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Filter messages containing: (Separator \| )** | see below | Words, apart by `\|`: a message that holds one of them anywhere, in upper or lower case, is left out, from friends too. At most 4,096 characters; empty filters nothing. Key `chat.filter`. Also in the web interface. |
| **Accept from friends only** | Off | Shows only the messages of your friends. Key `chat.friendsOnly`. Also in the web interface. |
| **Advanced spam filter** | On | Leaves out the usual spam of clients that are not your friends. See below. Key `chat.spamFilter`. Also in the web interface. |

The default words are:

```text
fastest download speed|fastest eMule
```

## Advanced spam filter

With **Advanced spam filter** on, njMule watches each conversation with a client that is not your
friend. Until you write to that client:

- a message with a link (`http:`, `https:`, `ftp:`, `ed2k:`, `www.` or `ftp.`) is left out;
- the first three messages show; a fourth one is left out;
- a message with a filtered word is left out too.

Any of these marks the client as a spammer: from then on, nothing it sends shows. Write to a client
before that happens, and these checks no longer apply to it; only the filtered words still do.
