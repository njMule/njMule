The words you meet in njMule and on the eD2K and Kad networks, in alphabetical order. Many come
from eMule, whose terms njMule keeps so that people who know it feel at home.

## A

### A4AF

*Asked for another file.* A client that is a source of several of your downloads is asked for one
of them at a time; the others list it with this state. njMule moves it to the download that needs
it most, and you can move such sources by hand. See [Transfers](desktop/transfers.mdx).

### AICH

*Advanced Intelligent Corruption Handling.* A tree of SHA-1 hashes over blocks of 180 KiB of a
file, whose root can travel in the file's ed2k link (`h=`). When a [part](#part) fails its check,
njMule asks a source for the AICH recovery data of that part and downloads again only the blocks
that are bad, instead of the whole part. Without a root in the link, njMule takes the one most
sources agree on.

### API token

The secret that programs send to njMule's remote-control API, kept in the file `api-token` of the
configuration folder. `njmule --api-token` prints it, `njmule --new-api-token` replaces it. See
[Remote control](server/remote-control.mdx).

## B

### Block

A piece of 180 KiB (184,320 bytes) of a file: the unit in which data is asked for and in which
[AICH](#aich) checks it. A [part](#part) holds 53 blocks, the last one shorter.

### Bootstrap

Joining Kad for the first time, or again after a long pause, from contacts you give it: a
`nodes.dat` from the web or from a file, or one client you know by its address and UDP port. See
[Servers and Kad](network/servers-and-kad.mdx).

### Buddy

A Kad node that other nodes can reach and that keeps a link open to a [firewalled](#firewalled)
one. Others who want a file from the firewalled node ask the buddy, which passes the request on so
that the firewalled node connects to them. njMule finds a buddy when it is firewalled, and serves as
the buddy of one firewalled node when it is reachable. See [NAT traversal](network/nat-traversal.mdx).

## C

### Callback

Asking a client that others cannot reach to connect to you instead. On eD2K the server passes the
request on; on Kad a [buddy](#buddy) does, or a direct UDP message when the client's UDP port is
open.

### Category

A group of downloads with a name, a tint, an incoming folder and a priority of its own, and file
name patterns that sort new downloads into it. See [Categories](desktop/categories.mdx).

### Chunk

Another name for a [part](#part).

### Client

Any program on the eD2K and Kad networks that downloads and uploads files: njMule, eMule, aMule
and others. The transfers list the clients you upload to, those waiting for you and, under **Known
Clients**, those met in the last 90 days.

### Comments and ratings

What people say about a file and how they rate it, from **Invalid / Corrupt / Fake** to
**Excellent**. Your comment on a
shared file goes to the clients that download it and to Kad, where it is called a *note*; njMule
shows the comments of a download's sources and those found on Kad. See
[Transfers](desktop/transfers.mdx).

### Credits

What a client earned with you by uploading to you. In your upload queue, its waiting time is
multiplied by a factor from 1 to 10 that grows with what it gave you compared with what it got, so
clients that shared with you are served sooner; others do the same for you. Credits are kept by
[user hash](#user-hash) in `clients.met`, bound to the client's
[secure identification](#secure-identification-sui), and forgotten after 150 days without contact.

## E

### eD2K

The eDonkey2000 network: servers that know which files users share, and clients that exchange the
files directly with one another. njMule speaks its protocol, as eMule does. See
[Servers and Kad](network/servers-and-kad.mdx).

### ed2k link

A text that names a file on eD2K, starting with `ed2k://`: its name, size and [hash](#file-hash),
as in `ed2k://|file|name|size|hash|/`, optionally with an [AICH](#aich) root (`h=`), part hashes
(`p=`) or sources. Links can also name a server, a server list or a list of Kad nodes. See
[eD2K links](desktop/ed2k-links.mdx).

### eMule

The best-known client of eD2K and Kad, whose protocols, file formats and interface texts njMule
follows. njMule is not affiliated with the eMule project.

## F

### File hash

The 128-bit MD4 hash that identifies a file on eD2K, written as 32 hexadecimal digits: the MD4
hash of the data for a file smaller than one [part](#part), and of the parts' own MD4 hashes for
a larger one. Two files with the same hash have the same
content, whatever their names.

### Firewalled

Kad's word for not reachable from outside: other nodes cannot connect to your TCP port, or send to
your UDP port (*UDP firewalled*). It is the Kad counterpart of a [LowID](#highid-and-lowid). See
[Ports and firewalls](network/ports.mdx).

### Friend slot

An upload slot given to one friend for the current run: the friend goes before everyone waiting in
the queue, also when the queue is full, and keeps the slot. See [Messages](desktop/messages.mdx).

## G

### Global search

A search sent over UDP to the other known servers besides the one you are connected to. The
*Automatic* method picks Kad or the server by what is connected. See [Search](desktop/search.mdx).

### Guest

A second password of the web interface that lets someone look at the transfers, searches, servers,
Kad and statistics without changing anything. See [Guest access](web/guest.mdx).

## H

### Hash set

The list of the MD4 hashes of a file's [parts](#part), which lets a download check each part as
soon as it is complete.

### HighID and LowID

The ID a server gives you. When the server can connect to your TCP port, you get a *HighID*,
derived from your IPv4 address. When it cannot, you get a *LowID*, a number below 16,777,216: other
clients cannot connect to you directly and must ask through the server (a [callback](#callback)),
and two LowID clients usually cannot reach each other on eD2K. See
[Ports and firewalls](network/ports.mdx).

## I

### ICH

*Intelligent corruption handling.* A part that fails its check keeps its data while it is
downloaded again and is checked after each block written into it, so it is whole again as soon as
the bad block came in again, even without [AICH](#aich).

### Incoming folder

Where finished downloads go. It is always shared. Set it in **Options → Directories**
(**Incoming Files**); a [category](#category) may have one of its own. See
[Data locations](data/locations.mdx).

### IP filter

A list of address ranges njMule never talks to, read from `ipfilter.dat`, PeerGuardian or P2B
lists and optionally updated from the web. Each range has a level, and ranges below the filter
level (127 by default) block. Addresses under **Always allowed** are never blocked. See
[IP filter](network/ip-filter.mdx).

## K

### Kad

The serverless network of eMule, based on Kademlia: every client is a node that keeps contacts to
others and stores a share of the index of keywords, sources and notes. It finds files and sources
without any server. njMule speaks Kad 2. See [Servers and Kad](network/servers-and-kad.mdx).

### Kad ID

Your 128-bit identifier on Kad, kept in `preferencesKad.dat`. The distance between two IDs decides
which nodes store what, and which contacts the Kad view draws as near or far.

### Known files

The files njMule has hashed, downloaded or shared, with their hashes, kept in `known.met` (and the
AICH trees in `known2_64.met`), so that they are not hashed again. Search results show the files
you downloaded before; cancelled ones are remembered in `cancelled.met`.

## L

### LowID

See [HighID and LowID](#highid-and-lowid).

## M

### Magnet link

A link starting with `magnet:?`. njMule takes those that carry an eD2K hash (`urn:ed2k:` or
`urn:ed2khash:`) as it takes an [ed2k link](#ed2k-link).

### MiniMule

The small status window that opens with a click on njMule's icon in the Windows notification
area: connection, rates, finished downloads and free space. See [Notification area](desktop/tray.mdx).

## N

### NAT traversal

Connecting two clients that are both behind routers: each side sends packets that open its router
to the other, then they talk over [uTP or QUIC](#utp-and-quic). See
[NAT traversal](network/nat-traversal.mdx).

### Nick

The name others see in their upload lists and in chats; `njMule` until you choose one. Set it in
**Options → General**.

### nodes.dat

The file of Kad contacts, in the configuration folder: Kad starts from it, and a fresh one from
the web brings Kad back after a long pause. See [Bootstrap](#bootstrap).

## O

### Obfuscation

Protocol obfuscation scrambles the traffic with servers and clients so that simple filters cannot
recognise eD2K and Kad. It is not encryption and hides nothing from those you talk to. It can be
**Disabled**, **Supported**, **Requested** (the default) or **Required**. See
[Obfuscation](network/obfuscation.mdx).

### Overhead

The bytes of the protocol itself, besides the file data: requests, answers, searches and Kad
traffic. The statistics show it apart.

## P

### Part

A piece of 9,728,000 bytes of a file, called a *chunk* in eMule; the last part of a file is
shorter. Each part has its own MD4 hash and is checked as soon as it is complete; only checked
parts are shared with others.

### Part file

An unfinished download in the temporary folder: the data in `001.part` (or another number) and
what is known about it in `001.part.met`, the format eMule uses. See [Files](data/files.mdx).

### Portable mode

njMule keeps all its data in a `data` folder next to the programs when an empty `portable.flag`
file lies beside them. See [Portable mode](data/portable.mdx).

### Preview

Opening what has arrived of a video in the player of your choice before it is complete; njMule can
fetch the first and last parts first for it. See [Transfers](desktop/transfers.mdx).

### Priority

How much a file is favoured. A download's priority (**Low**, **Normal**, **High** or **Auto**,
which favours rare files) decides which downloads look for sources and start first. A shared
file's upload priority, from **Very Low** to **Release**, or **Auto**, scales the place in your
queue of the clients that want it. See [Release priority](#release-priority).

### Profile

All of njMule's own data on a computer: its configuration, identity, lists, unfinished downloads,
caches and logs, spread over the folders of [Data locations](data/locations.mdx). `--config-dir`
starts njMule on another profile.

## Q

### Queue rank

Your place in the upload queue of a source: 1 is next. It changes as others join or leave and with
the [credits](#credits) each client has there. While you wait the source shows **On Queue**;
**Queue Full** means it takes nobody more for now. See
[Downloads stay "Waiting"](troubleshooting.mdx#downloads-stay-waiting).

## R

### Release priority

The highest upload priority of a shared file, meant for a file you are the first to share: its
waiting clients count more than twice as much as those of a file of **Normal** priority.

## S

### Scheduler

Rules by weekday and time that change the speed limits and connections, and can stop or resume the
downloads of a category. See [Scheduler](settings/scheduler.mdx).

### Secure identification (SUI)

A proof, with an RSA key kept in `cryptkey.dat`, that a client really owns its
[user hash](#user-hash). It keeps others from taking your [credits](#credits). **Use secure
identification** turns it off.

### Server

A computer of the eD2K network that knows which files its users share. It answers searches and
tells where a file's sources are, but holds no files. The list of servers is kept in
[server.met](#servermet). See [Servers and Kad](network/servers-and-kad.mdx).

### server.met

The file of known eD2K servers, in the configuration folder. A new profile starts with the list
shipped with njMule; more can come from a server.met on the web or in a file.

### Source

A client that has all or part of a file you download. Each source shows what it does:
**Transferring**, **On Queue**, **Connecting**, **Asking**, **Queue Full**, **No needed parts** or
[A4AF](#a4af).

### Source exchange

Clients telling each other the other sources they know of a file they share, so that sources spread
without asking a server or Kad.

### Spam filter

Two filters. The search spam filter rates search results by what you marked as spam before (names,
sizes, hashes and the servers that sent them) and shows the spam muted. The message filters leave
out chat messages with filtered words or links. See [IP filter options](settings/ip-filter.mdx) and
[Message options](settings/messages.mdx).

### Static server

A server you keep in the list for good: it is never removed as dead, and automatic connection can
keep to static servers only. They are kept in `staticservers.dat`.

## T

### Temp folder

Where unfinished downloads are kept as [part files](#part-file) until they are complete; called
**Temporary Files** in **Options → Directories**. See [Data locations](data/locations.mdx).

## U

### Upload queue

The clients waiting for a turn to download from you, ordered by how long they waited, their
[credits](#credits) and the [priority](#priority) of the file they want; up to 5,000 by default
(**Queue size**). They show under **On Queue** in the transfers.

### Upload slot

A turn to download from you. njMule opens as many slots as the upload limit allows and shares the
bandwidth among them; a client keeps its slot for about one part, then the next one in the queue
gets it.

### UPnP, NAT-PMP and PCP

Ways for njMule to ask your router to forward its ports, so others can reach you. njMule tries UPnP
first (**Forward the ports with UPnP**, on by default), then NAT-PMP or PCP, and removes the
forwarding when it exits. See [Ports and firewalls](network/ports.mdx).

### User hash

Your 128-bit identity on eD2K, made at the first start and kept in `preferences.dat`. Clients
remember your [credits](#credits) under it. Never share it, and never run two clients with the
same one at once.

### uTP and QUIC

Two ways to carry a connection over UDP, used for [NAT traversal](#nat-traversal) between
firewalled clients: QUIC, encrypted with TLS 1.3, with clients that take it, otherwise uTP. See
[NAT traversal](network/nat-traversal.mdx).
