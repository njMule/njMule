The look and the language of the desktop app are set in **Options** → **General** → **Display**.
Both start as your system is: njMule follows its light or dark scheme and speaks its language when it
is one of the 36 it has.

## Theme

**Theme** has two lists:

1. **The family**: **Nord** (the default), **Primer**, **Cupertino** or **Dracula**. Dracula exists
   in a dark version only.
2. **The mode**: **Light**, **Dark**, or **As the system** (the default), which follows the light or
   dark scheme of your system as it changes.

A change applies at once, to every window of njMule. **⋯** → **Light or dark** (also in the command
palette) switches straight between the light and the dark version of your theme; it sets **Light**
or **Dark**, so choose **As the system** in the options to follow the system again. The link **Reset
to Nord, As the system** beside a changed theme brings the default back.

Whatever the theme, njMule keeps its own soft colours for the bars, charts and chips, in a light and a
dark version, and makes sure text and marks stay readable: every theme is checked for the contrast of
what njMule draws on it (WCAG 2.2 AA). Colour is never the only cue: states have words and icons,
legends name what each colour means.

## Windows high contrast

When a high contrast theme of Windows is on, njMule takes the system's colours for everything: the
window, the text, the selection, the links, the greyed-out text, the part bars and the graphs. It is
light or dark as the high contrast theme is, whatever the mode chosen, and follows the system at once
when the theme changes or is turned off; your own choice of theme comes back then.

## Language

**Language** lists **As the system** (the default) and the 36 languages, each named in its own words
(Deutsch, Italiano, 日本語…). A new language waits for **Apply and restart** in the bar under the page
(or **Discard**): the window is then built again in that language, while the transfers go on.

<details>
<summary>The 36 languages</summary>

English, Catalan, Czech, Danish, German, Estonian, Spanish, French, Galician, Italian, Latvian,
Lithuanian, Hungarian, Dutch, Norwegian Bokmål, Polish, Brazilian Portuguese, European Portuguese,
Romanian, Albanian, Slovenian, Finnish, Swedish, Vietnamese, Turkish, Greek, Bulgarian, Russian,
Ukrainian, Arabic, Persian, Hebrew, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Japanese and Korean.

</details>

With **As the system**, njMule speaks your system's language when it has it, and English otherwise:

- Chinese written in the traditional script (as set for Taiwan, Hong Kong or Macao) gets Traditional
  Chinese, any other Chinese Simplified Chinese.
- Portuguese gets Brazilian Portuguese in Brazil and European Portuguese elsewhere.
- Norwegian and Nynorsk get Norwegian Bokmål.

Sizes, rates and durations use the units of the language, as `Ko/s` in French; numbers always use
Latin digits, Persian included. Dates and times follow the format of your system.

### Right-to-left languages

In Arabic, Persian and Hebrew the whole interface is mirrored: the window, its dialogs, the menus and
the windows of the notification area. What reads left to right in any language stays so: paths,
links, hashes, addresses, the numbers in lists, the logs, and the fields for links and addresses.
File and user names read in the direction of their first letter. The part bars and the rate graph
keep their direction, the start of a file and the past on the left; of the icons, only those that
point the way text runs are mirrored.

## Fonts and icons

Titles and the njMule wordmark are set in Manrope, which comes with njMule. All other text uses your
system's font, which covers every script of the translations. The icons are njMule's own line icons,
drawn in the colour of the text beside them in every theme.

## Screen readers

Screen readers hear the progress bars as progress with their value, the state chips, the file names
and the outcomes in the lists as words, and the rate graph of the statistics as a sentence with the
rates now and the highest.
